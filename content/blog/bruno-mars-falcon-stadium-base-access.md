Almost every stadium on The Romantic Tour works the way stadiums usually do: you buy a ticket, you turn up, you are screened at the gate, you go in. Two dates do not. Bruno Mars' official tour listing puts them at "Falcon Stadium at the United States Air Force Academy" in Colorado, and the stadium is inside a working military installation. Getting to the stadium is a separate permission from getting into the show, and it is granted by the Academy rather than by anyone selling you a ticket.

## What the Academy's own policy requires

The Academy publishes its visitor rules, and they are the authority here — not a ticket site, not this page. Its Getting On Base guidance states that all visitors without a Department of Defense ID card must have a sponsored pass, and that visitors aged 18 or over must present REAL ID-compliant identification for entry along with that issued pass when entering the gate. Access is via the North Gate (Interstate-25 Exit 156) or the South Gate (Exit 150), described as open to sponsored visitors from 5:30am to 10pm every day. The policy also states that all individuals seeking access undergo a mandatory criminal background check.

There is a fallback for people whose driver's licence is not REAL ID-compliant: the Academy's guidance says US citizens without a REAL ID licence must visit the Pass and Registration Office in person with a valid passport. That office is at the South Gate, open weekdays 7:30am to 3pm, and the Academy's own wording warns that waits of one to two hours can be expected at peak periods. A weekday, daytime, in-person office visit is not a plan you can execute on a Saturday evening when the show starts at seven.

## Why this is a buying decision, not just a logistics one

Ordinarily the gap between "I have a ticket" and "I am inside" is a queue. Here it includes a document requirement, a registration step and a background check, and none of those are things a ticket seller controls or can fix for you at the gate.

That has three practical consequences. Everyone in your group needs their own compliant identification, so a ticket bought for someone else is only useful if that person can also clear the gate. The arrangements take lead time, so the moment to check is when you are deciding to buy, not the week of the show. And because the gate hours and the show time are set independently, the travel plan has to satisfy both.

The Academy's published guidance is general visitor policy. Whether a specific concert has its own registration link, its own gate timings, or its own instructions is for the event's own listing and the Academy to say. Read the listing for your date, then read the Academy's guidance, and treat any conflict between a third-party page and the Academy as settled in the Academy's favour.

## What the ticket side of this looks like

Live Nation's listing for the Falcon Stadium date names RAYE and DJ Pee .Wee as special guests. RAYE has [her own page here](/artists/raye), though it currently carries no upcoming dates of her own that we have verified — a support billing on someone else's tour is not a headline date, and we do not list it as one.

On our [Bruno Mars page](/artists/bruno-mars) these two dates behave like any other: they appear with the ticket sites that have a checked destination for that exact date, and with a listed-price snapshot where an approved provider lane has a fresh one. What our page cannot do is tell you anything about base access, because that is not ticket data and we do not hold it. It is also why "can I get in" and "do I have a ticket" are worth keeping as two separate questions for these two dates only.

One more thing worth knowing before you commit: if a show at an unusual site is moved or called off, the refund path runs through whoever sold you the ticket, not the venue. The [guide to postponed and cancelled concerts](/guides/what-to-do-if-a-concert-is-postponed-or-cancelled) covers who owes you what, and it is a more useful thing to have read in advance than afterwards.
