Most days, the cheapest resale ticket for a concert costs the same as it did the day before. But when it moves, it can move a lot. TourTicketCompare keeps a daily record of the lowest listed price on Vivid Seats, TicketNetwork and StubHub International for the shows it tracks. For US shows with at least 30 days of prices between 1 September and 2 November 2026, the lowest listing was unchanged from one day to the next about two times in three. Yet at about half of shows it jumped or fell by 20% or more overnight at least once.

## Site by site

Each row covers one site's own listings. "Unchanged" means within half a percent of the day before.

| Site | Shows | Artists | Days unchanged | Shows with a 20%+ overnight move | Shows where the high was 2× the low |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vivid Seats | 1,195 | 79 | 66% | 560 (47%) | 217 (18%) |
| TicketNetwork | 1,233 | 82 | 71% | 844 (68%) | 347 (28%) |
| StubHub International | 533 | 58 | 63% | 315 (59%) | 130 (24%) |

No single artist made up more than about 10% of any site's shows. Sites list different seats with different fees, so this does not compare one site with another.

## What it looks like

Vivid Seats, lowest listed price at the last check of the day (days in UTC):

- **Gracie Abrams, Inglewood, 19 December 2026.** About $90 on 1 September 2026, down to $32 on 23 October.
- **Gracie Abrams, Oakland, 6 December 2026.** About $99 on 2 September 2026, down to $33 on 31 October.

Those two fell, but big moves went both ways. A price that drops overnight can come back the next day.

## The second night of a run is not cheaper

Some fans assume the extra date added to a sold-out run is the cheap one. TourTicketCompare compared back-to-back nights by the same artist at the same venue, using each night's typical lowest listing in October 2026. On Vivid Seats, the second night was cheaper in 85 of 223 pairs and dearer in 102. On TicketNetwork it was cheaper in 96 of 210 and dearer in 80, and on StubHub International cheaper in 77 of 193 and dearer in 94. There was no consistent pattern either way.

## What this does and doesn't say

- **It is the lowest listed price, not every seat.** A big move can be one cheap listing appearing or selling, not every ticket changing price.
- **It is daily, not live.** Prices can change several times a day. This records the last check of each day.
- **It is not a forecast.** It shows how often prices moved, not which way the next move will go.
- **It is US shows only,** and covers September to early November 2026.
- **A listed price is not a checkout total.** Fees, delivery and seat location differ between listings and between sites.

Prices below $5 or above $1,200 were left out as placeholders or outliers.

Because prices can change overnight, check the time next to any price before you buy. For the current listed price of any show, open the artist's page. The [guide to why ticket prices change](/guides/why-ticket-prices-change) explains what moves them.
