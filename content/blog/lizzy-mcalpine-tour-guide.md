## What moved over the last 30 days

Most listings with a 30-day history moved down.

- London: StubHub International went from USD 110 to USD 102, TicketNetwork from USD 162 to USD 148, and Vivid Seats from USD 231 to USD 186.
- Minneapolis: TicketNetwork went from USD 386 to USD 217, and Vivid Seats from USD 387 to USD 198.
- Glasgow: TicketNetwork went from USD 629 to USD 275.
- Birmingham: StubHub International went from USD 190 to USD 159.
- Houston: StubHub International went from USD 64 to USD 60.
- Detroit: TicketNetwork went from USD 176 to USD 146.

A few went the other way. Both Indianapolis listings rose: TicketNetwork from USD 150 to USD 168, and Vivid Seats from USD 156 to USD 171. In Detroit, Vivid Seats rose from USD 137 to USD 160. In Houston, TicketNetwork rose from USD 66 to USD 91 and Vivid Seats from USD 74 to USD 76.

Treat this as a record of where listings sat, not a hint about where they go next. A falling line last month is no promise about next month.

## Check the Houston Vivid Seats listing first

The Vivid Seats figure of USD 76 for Houston on 2027-05-01 sits far below that site's figures for the other dates in this table. That can be a real listing, or it can be something with conditions attached. Open it on Vivid Seats and check the section, row, quantity and delivery details before you rely on it. The guide on [how to read a ticket listing](/guides/how-to-read-a-ticket-listing) covers what to look for.

## Picking your date

A lower listed price only helps if you can get to the show. If you would need to travel, settle the city first, then compare listings for that exact date.

The guide to [primary vs resale concert tickets](/guides/primary-vs-resale-concert-tickets) explains how a resale listing differs from a ticket bought from the primary seller. If you are booking travel around a show, read up on [ticket delivery and transfer timing](/guides/ticket-delivery-and-transfer-timing) so you know when the tickets should reach you.
