Each opens at 10:00 in the venue's own time zone, so the sale rolls from the East Coast at 10:00 AM EDT to the West Coast three hours later.

## Why resale listings came first

On September 24, the day before the public on-sale, TourTicketCompare checked resale links for all ten dates, at sites including Vivid Seats, TicketNetwork and StubHub International. That is normal for a big stadium announcement. It is also worth pausing on, because a listing that exists before the public sale opened was not bought in that sale.

It might come from a presale, or it might be a seller's promise to buy and deliver a ticket later. Either way, the questions are the same:

- **When will the ticket arrive?** Read the delivery or transfer date on the listing. A date close to the show is common for resale.
- **What exactly is it?** Section, row, whether seats are together, and whether it is floor or seated. Stadium floor and stadium seats are different products.
- **What will it cost in total?** Resale prices are set by sellers and can sit well above face value. Compare the checkout total, not the first number.
- **What does the site cover?** Read the resale site's own protection terms for your order.

The [primary versus resale guide](/guides/primary-vs-resale-concert-tickets) sets out the differences. The [delivery and transfer guide](/guides/ticket-delivery-and-transfer-timing) explains why a resale ticket often arrives late.

## Once the public sale has opened

After each date's on-sale time, the Metallica page shows the Ticketmaster link for that date alongside any checked resale links. If the official sale still has seats that suit you, that is face value from the authorised seller. If it doesn't, the resale links are there. Match the stadium, date, section and quantity on each site before comparing totals.

The [Metallica page](/artists/metallica) lists every tracked date, including the Sphere residency and Mohegan Sun.
