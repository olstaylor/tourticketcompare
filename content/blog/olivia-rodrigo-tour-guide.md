## The weekday changes the whole plan

Oakland on [2026-12-11](https://www.ticketmaster.com/olivia-rodrigo-the-unraveled-tour-oakland-california-12-11-2026/event/1C00649DD84D193A) is a Friday. It is the only Friday in the table, so check the listed start time and plan your travel around it.

Everything else lands midweek. Seattle and London are Mondays, Vancouver, Sacramento and Inglewood are Tuesdays, and Brooklyn is a Thursday. If you are travelling, a midweek show usually means booking time off on both sides of it, so add that to the cost of the date before you compare tickets.

## Country and ticket site

The Vancouver listing sits on [Ticketmaster's Canadian site](https://www.ticketmaster.ca/olivia-rodrigo-the-unraveled-tour-vancouver-british-columbia-12-01-2026/event/1100649D82B16018) and the London listing on [Ticketmaster's UK site](https://www.ticketmaster.co.uk/olivia-rodrigo-the-unraveled-tour-london-05-04-2027/event/3500649DB098427C). The other dates use the US site. Before paying for a show outside your own country, check which currency the listing is charged in, what delivery method it uses and which terms apply. Do that on the ticket site itself, not from memory of a different country's checkout.

## Time zones and venue-local dates

Every date here is the date at the venue. Seattle, Oakland, Sacramento and Inglewood share the America/Los_Angeles time zone, Vancouver uses America/Vancouver, Brooklyn uses America/New_York and London uses Europe/London. If you are buying London from North America, or Brooklyn from the West Coast, read the listing's date as local to the venue and plan travel around that.

The California dates are also a useful fallback group. If Oakland clashes with something, Sacramento and Inglewood are other dates in the same state and time zone, on different weeks.

## Making two listings the same ticket

The same show can appear on more than one ticket site, and listing titles are often worded differently. Ignore the title and match the facts instead: venue name, city and venue-local date should all agree with the official event page linked in the table. Searching by venue as well as city helps where a site labels the location more broadly than the city shown here.

Once the event matches, compare the seat or section, the number of tickets, whether the listing is from the primary seller or a resale marketplace, and how the ticket is delivered. The guides to [reading a ticket listing](/guides/how-to-read-a-ticket-listing), [official versus resale tickets](/guides/primary-vs-resale-concert-tickets) and [delivery and transfer timing](/guides/ticket-delivery-and-transfer-timing) cover each of those checks in detail.

## A short checklist before paying

- Confirm the date, venue and city against the Ticketmaster page in the table.
- Count the days off and travel the weekday will need.
- Check the currency and terms if the show is in another country.
- Match section, quantity and ticket type across every listing you compare.
- Read the full order total, fees and delivery terms on the ticket site before you confirm.
