The word "price" does a lot of unearned work on ticket sites. It can mean a starting figure, a per-ticket figure before fees, a figure for a section that has two seats left, or the number you actually pay. When a figure appears on an event card here, it means something specific and quite narrow, and it is worth knowing exactly what.

## One provider, one event, one moment

A snapshot is a listed price a single provider published for a single verified show, recorded with the time it was captured. It is stored, displayed with that timestamp, and expires. It is not an average, not a market rate, and not a figure we calculated.

Several conditions all have to hold before a figure renders at all: the provider must have granted display rights, the record must map to that exact event rather than a nearby one, the destination URL must be independently verified, and the observation has to be recent enough to still be meaningful. If any one fails, no figure appears. The absence of a price on a card is a real signal, not a rendering bug.

## Why snapshots expire

Listed prices move continuously. Measuring our own stored history, they drift by low single-digit percentages per hour across the resale lanes — enough that a figure captured this morning is genuinely a different claim by this evening. Snapshots are therefore refreshed on a short cycle and carry a hard expiry: past it, the figure stops displaying rather than sitting there looking current.

That is also why the site shows a capture time next to a price instead of presenting it as the price. The timestamp is not a disclaimer bolted on afterwards; it is the part that makes the number honest.

## Not every provider has one

Providers differ in what their feeds expose, and we do not fill the gaps by guessing. Some approved partners supply numeric listed prices and can show a figure. At least one supplies verified links but no usable numeric price at all, so it is a link-only provider here and always will be. Ticketmaster is a verification and link source on this site rather than a price lane.

The visible consequence is asymmetry: one show may display a figure from two providers and a plain link from a third. That is the data being reported accurately rather than flattened into a tidier-looking table.

## What we will never tell you

We will not rank providers against each other or crown one of them the low-price option. Snapshots are per provider and per moment; ordering them as if they were like-for-like offers would imply a comparison the underlying data cannot support. We also make no claim about availability, because we have no inventory feed and inventing one would be worse than saying nothing.

What a snapshot is good for is narrowing the field before you open two or three tabs. The final number is the provider's order summary after fees, delivery, and tax, and [our guide to ticket fees](/guides/concert-ticket-fees-explained) covers what tends to get added between the headline and the total.
