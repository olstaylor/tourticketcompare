When a ticket button here shows a price, it's the lowest listed price one ticket site had for that show, at the time it was checked. That's a smaller promise than "price" usually means. On ticket sites, "price" can mean a starting figure, a price before fees, the price of the last two seats in a section, or what you actually pay.

## One site, one show, one moment

Each price comes from one ticket site, for one show, at one time. It isn't an average, a market rate, or your final total. Some fees may already be included. Check the full price for the number of tickets you want, with taxes, delivery and any extras, on the ticket site before you pay.

A price only shows if the ticket site allows it, it's for that exact show (not a nearby date), the link to the show has been checked, and it's recent enough. If any of those fails, there's no price on the button. A missing price is the site being careful, not a glitch.

## Why prices don't stay up forever

Prices move. A number with no time on it soon becomes a promise nobody can keep. So prices are re-checked through the day, each one shows how long ago it was checked ("Checked 5 hours ago"), and once one is more than 24 hours old it comes down.

That's why every price here has its age next to it. The time isn't small print. It's what makes the number trustworthy.

## Not every ticket site has a price

Ticket sites share different things. Vivid Seats, TicketNetwork and StubHub International share their listed prices and allow them to be shown, so their buttons can carry one. SeatGeek doesn't share prices with this site, so its button is always a plain checked link. Ticket Liquidator doesn't share a price either. Ticketmaster is a plain link here.

So one show might have prices from two sites and plain links to three more. That's an honest picture, not a messy one.

## What the button order means

Buttons always appear in the same order: sites that pay this site a commission first, Ticketmaster (which pays nothing) last. The page says so next to the buttons. It isn't a ranking, and nothing sorts by price or points at a "best" option.

That's because prices checked at different times aren't a fair head-to-head. One price is only called lower than another when both are fresh and for the same show. And nothing here claims tickets are still available: the site can't see how many are left, and it won't pretend to.

What a price here is good for is a quick shortlist before you open two or three tabs. Your real total is on the ticket site's order screen, after fees, delivery and tax. [The guide to ticket fees](/guides/concert-ticket-fees-explained) explains what usually gets added along the way.
