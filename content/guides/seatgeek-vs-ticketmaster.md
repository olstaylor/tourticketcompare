Start with the seller linked by the artist or venue. That may be Ticketmaster, SeatGeek or another ticket site. If you need resale tickets, compare the seats and full order total: neither brand is always the cheaper choice.

Ticketmaster sells primary tickets for many events and also offers labelled resale tickets. SeatGeek is a resale marketplace and the primary ticketing partner for some venues and teams. The distinction matters more than the logo on the checkout page.

## Is SeatGeek cheaper than Ticketmaster?

Sometimes, but a lower starting price alone tells you little. A SeatGeek resale ticket can cost less than a Ticketmaster premium ticket, or more than a primary ticket released at face value. A different row, restricted view or minimum purchase quantity can also explain the gap.

For example, two tickets advertised at the same per-ticket price need not cost the same overall. One listing might require you to buy four together; another might charge separately for delivery. Match the quantity and read the order summary before comparing.

In the US, mandatory fees must be included in upfront ticket prices under the FTC's rule. Ticketmaster says its upfront price includes face value and required fees, generally before taxes. SeatGeek says its event-page price includes mandatory fees; taxes, shipping and optional extras may still be added at checkout. Rules and displays can differ outside the US. The [fees guide](/guides/concert-ticket-fees-explained) explains the details.

On TTC, Ticketmaster is a plain, unmonetised link source. SeatGeek starting prices appear only where TTC has a checked price for that event. A missing figure means you need to check the seller's page; it does not establish ticket availability. TTC may earn a commission from SeatGeek links, at no extra cost to you.

## Why does one site have tickets when the other does not?

Ticketmaster's primary allocation can sell out while resale tickets remain listed elsewhere. SeatGeek sellers may be offering tickets bought earlier, including tickets that will later transfer through Ticketmaster. The reverse happens too: Ticketmaster may have tickets that are not listed on SeatGeek.

Check Ticketmaster's resale labels as well as its primary tickets. Artists and venues decide whether and when their tickets can be resold there. Some events use Face Value Exchange, which caps resale prices; Ticketmaster says it charges no resale fees on those tickets. Transfer restrictions can also limit where a ticket can safely be resold.

## What does SeatGeek Deal Score tell you?

Deal Score compares listings within SeatGeek using factors including price, row, expected sightline, quantity and other available tickets. SeatGeek gives listings a score from 1 to 10; a higher score indicates better value within its own comparison.

A blue map pin means there was not enough information to calculate a score. It is not, by itself, a warning about the ticket. Deal Score cannot tell you whether the equivalent Ticketmaster ticket costs less.

## Buyer protection and delivery

SeatGeek's Buyer Guarantee covers tickets arriving in time, providing valid entry and matching the order. Its published terms provide for comparable or better replacements or a refund if these conditions fail. For a cancelled event that is not rescheduled, SeatGeek says it may refund the purchase price or, subject to state law, issue credit. Postponed or rescheduled events generally do not qualify for a refund. Mistaken purchases, changes to your plans and lineup changes are excluded.

Ticketmaster handles primary and resale orders under event-specific rules. Read the terms attached to the ticket, especially cancellation, postponement and transfer restrictions. A familiar platform name does not make every purchase refundable.


## Before you buy: one comparison checklist

1. Match the artist, venue and local date, then identify primary, premium or resale tickets.
2. Match the quantity, section, row, view restrictions and any minimum purchase requirement.
3. Compare the full order totals in the same currency, including taxes and delivery. Check conversion charges if paying in another currency.
4. Check when and how tickets will arrive, whether transfers are allowed, and the protection and refund terms.
5. Pay through the ticket site's own checkout. Avoid requests to move payment elsewhere.

If the listings differ, treat them as different options and compare their details before judging value. For an official onsale, start with the authorised seller. For resale, choose the ticket and terms that fit your plans and budget.

## FAQ

**Is SeatGeek owned by Ticketmaster?**

No. They are separate businesses; Ticketmaster is part of Live Nation Entertainment. A SeatGeek purchase may still be delivered through Ticketmaster or AXS. Keep the SeatGeek order confirmation and follow the official transfer instructions. The delivery app does not change where you bought the ticket.

**Who should I contact if my order goes wrong?**

Contact the ticket site that took your payment, using its official app or website. Keep your order confirmation and any transfer emails. TTC does not sell tickets or handle orders.
