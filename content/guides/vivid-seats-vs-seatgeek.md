**Short answer:** Vivid Seats and SeatGeek are separate companies, and neither wins on price for every show. Vivid Seats is a resale marketplace where independent sellers set every price. SeatGeek is a resale marketplace too, and the primary ticketing platform for some leagues and venues. Both show all-in prices, each has a published buyer guarantee, and both treat a postponed show very differently from a cancelled one. Compare the same seats, for the same date, at the full order total.

## Vivid Seats vs SeatGeek at a glance

| Question | Vivid Seats | SeatGeek |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Resale marketplace | Primary platform for some leagues and venues, plus a resale marketplace |
| Who sets the price? | Independent resellers | The primary partner, or the seller on resale listings |
| Fee display | All-in pricing once you start shopping | All-in pricing: mandatory fees in the price shown |
| Protection name | Buyer Guarantee | Buyer Guarantee |
| Cancelled, not rescheduled | Refund or store credit of the purchase price, at Vivid Seats' discretion | Refund to the original payment method or, subject to state law, a credit |
| Postponed or rescheduled | Tickets valid for the new date | Tickets usually valid for the new date; generally no refund |

These are the terms each company publishes at the time of writing. The version you accept at checkout is the one that applies to your order.

## Who sets the price?

Vivid Seats says every ticket price on its site is set independently by the resellers who list there, based on demand for the event and the seat location. Prices can change at any time and can sit above or below the face value printed on the ticket. Sellers have to apply and be approved before they can list, and Vivid Seats marks its longest-standing sellers with a ribbon icon.

SeatGeek's listings come from licensed sellers, other SeatGeek customers, integrated partners and sometimes a team's box office. For the leagues and venues where SeatGeek is the primary platform, you may be buying a primary ticket rather than a resale one. Everywhere else it works as a marketplace, like Vivid Seats.

## Is Vivid Seats cheaper than SeatGeek?

Not reliably. When both are showing resale tickets, both prices come from sellers reacting to the same demand, and which one is lower changes from listing to listing and hour to hour. A lower number can also be a different section, a smaller quantity or a different delivery method.

Both sites display all-in prices, so the number you see already includes mandatory fees. SeatGeek says taxes, shipping and optional add-ons can still be added at checkout; check the final order summary on Vivid Seats in the same way. Then compare:

- The same section, row, quantity and ticket type.
- The same currency.
- The final order total, including taxes and delivery.
- When you checked, because resale listings move.

## Buyer protection

**Vivid Seats' Buyer Guarantee** promises a secure transaction, delivery before the event, and tickets that are valid and authentic.

**SeatGeek's Buyer Guarantee** covers tickets delivered in time, valid entry, and tickets matching what you ordered. If one of those fails, SeatGeek may provide comparable or better replacements or a refund, depending on the situation. It says the guarantee generally does not cover mistaken purchases, personal schedule changes, lineup changes that are not cancellations, being refused entry for breaking venue rules, or travel and hotel costs.

In practice both cover the same core failure: you paid, and the ticket did not get you in. Neither covers a change of plans.

## Cancelled and postponed shows

- **Cancelled and not rescheduled:** Vivid Seats compensates with a refund or store credit of the purchase price, including delivery fees, less possible restocking fees, at its discretion. SeatGeek refunds to the original payment method or, subject to state law, issues a credit.
- **Postponed or rescheduled:** both say your tickets stay valid for the new date. Vivid Seats notes that for some rescheduled events the original ticket seller reissues tickets with new barcodes, and Vivid Seats then delivers the new ones. SeatGeek says it generally does not refund postponed events unless replacement tickets are needed or the law requires it.

If a show you have tickets for changes, [what to do if a concert is postponed or cancelled](/guides/what-to-do-if-a-concert-is-postponed-or-cancelled) walks through the next steps.

## Delivery

Both marketplaces often deliver through another company's system, because the venue decides how tickets are issued. Vivid Seats says an electronic transfer may mean downloading another ticketing app to accept the tickets, and it emails an estimated delivery date once the order is confirmed. SeatGeek delivers some tickets straight to your SeatGeek account and others through apps such as AXS, and some are released only 24 to 48 hours before the event.

Neither is a sign of a problem on its own. Check the listed delivery method before you buy, and do not expect every ticket to appear straight after purchase.

## Rewards

Vivid Seats runs a loyalty scheme, Vivid Seats Rewards: its site says you earn a Reward Credit after your tenth ticket, to spend in its app. Treat rewards as a tiebreaker, not a reason to accept a worse seat or a higher total.

## Which should you use?

- **For an official onsale:** start with the seller named by the artist, venue or promoter. Vivid Seats is not a primary seller; SeatGeek sometimes is.
- **For resale:** open the same date on both and compare matching seats at the full order total.
- **If delivery timing matters:** pick the listing whose stated delivery method and date suit your plans.

## FAQ

**Is Vivid Seats the same as SeatGeek?**

No. They are separate companies. Vivid Seats is a resale marketplace; SeatGeek is a resale marketplace that is also the primary ticketing platform for some leagues and venues.

**Which is cheaper, Vivid Seats or SeatGeek?**

Neither, consistently. Resale prices are set by sellers and change often, so compare the same seats and quantity at the final order total on both sites at the same time.

**Do Vivid Seats and SeatGeek include fees in the price?**

Both say they use all-in pricing, so mandatory fees are in the price shown. Taxes, shipping and optional extras can still be added at checkout.

**What if my event is cancelled?**

If an event is cancelled and not rescheduled, Vivid Seats gives a refund or store credit and SeatGeek gives a refund or, subject to state law, a credit. Read the terms on your order for which applies.

**Why did Vivid Seats or SeatGeek ask me to use another app?**

Venues decide how tickets are issued. Some tickets are transferred through the venue's ticketing platform, so you accept them in that app rather than on the marketplace.

**Does TourTicketCompare rank Vivid Seats and SeatGeek?**

No. The site explains how they differ and links to them. It does not call either one better or cheaper in general.
