**Short answer:** Vivid Seats and SeatGeek are separate ticket marketplaces. Neither is cheaper or safer for every show. Vivid Seats is a resale marketplace where independent resellers set listing prices. SeatGeek is a resale marketplace too, but it is also the primary ticketing platform for some teams, venues and events. So for the same concert, the two can be selling different kinds of ticket.

Compare the listings for one exact date, not the two brands. Match the seat, quantity, delivery method and complete current total, then read each site's protection terms for that order.

On TourTicketCompare, a date covered by both shows a button for each. The Vivid Seats button can carry its lowest listed price for that date, with how long ago it was checked, when the Vivid Seats feed supplies one. The SeatGeek button never carries a price, because SeatGeek's feed returns no prices to this site. You'll finish the comparison on the two ticket sites.

These are typical roles for a US buyer. Roles, display rules and account requirements can differ by event and country, so read the exact listing and the current terms.

## How each site prices a listing

Vivid Seats says its resellers set listing prices using factors such as supply, demand, seat location and how close the event is. A price can sit above or below the ticket's original face value.

On SeatGeek, a listing can come from a fan or other eligible seller on the secondary market, or from a team, venue or partner that uses SeatGeek as its primary ticketing platform. SeatGeek's explanation of the secondary market describes both roles. Read the listing label so you know which you're looking at.

That difference matters. If SeatGeek is the primary seller for an event and Vivid Seats only has resale, a gap between them isn't a sign that one site is overcharging. They're selling different products.

## Fees and the total you actually pay

US federal rules require live-event ticket sellers to include mandatory fees in the price they first show. Taxes, shipping and optional extras can still be shown separately, as long as they're disclosed before you pay.

Both sites say they follow an upfront approach in the US. Vivid Seats says its all-in ticket price includes its service charge and delivery charge, with government tax and optional choices able to change the final amount. SeatGeek says mandatory fees are included, while taxes, delivery and optional services may be listed separately.

So compare the order summary, not the first figure. Use the same number of tickets and the same currency on both sites, and check that neither order has picked up an optional extra. The [concert ticket fees guide](/guides/concert-ticket-fees-explained) walks through the order summary line by line.

## Deal Score and Vivid Seats Rewards

Each site has a feature the other doesn't, and neither one settles which listing is better value.

- **SeatGeek Deal Score** rates a listing using factors such as historical prices, row location, expected sightline, quantity, seat type and other available tickets. It compares SeatGeek listings with one another. It doesn't compare SeatGeek with Vivid Seats or promise a lower price than another site.
- **Vivid Seats Rewards** is an app-based loyalty programme. Its current help page says eligible purchases earn stamps, and ten stamps can produce a Reward Credit based on the average eligible value of those tickets, excluding taxes, fees and processing costs. Credits follow the programme's current terms and can expire.

If rewards matter to you, value a future credit conservatively. It doesn't change today's total or make two different seats equivalent.

## Delivery timing

Many resale tickets arrive by mobile transfer, and the ticket often lives in the system of the company that issued it, not the marketplace you bought from. A Vivid Seats or SeatGeek order for a Ticketmaster-issued show may arrive as a transfer to your Ticketmaster account.

Transfers aren't always instant. Vivid Seats explains that sellers may not receive tickets from the original issuer until closer to the event, so the estimated delivery date can be later than the purchase date. On SeatGeek, check the delivery details shown on the listing and in the order. Read the delivery estimate on each listing before you pay, and treat a later delivery date as part of the comparison. See [ticket delivery and transfer timing](/guides/ticket-delivery-and-transfer-timing) for what to do when a transfer arrives.

## Buyer protection

Both sites publish order-protection terms. Vivid Seats publishes a Buyer Guarantee covering transaction security, delivery before the event, and ticket validity and authenticity, subject to its current terms and exclusions. SeatGeek publishes its own buyer-protection terms, and its help centre explains when tickets sold on SeatGeek can be trusted.

A published guarantee isn't the same as a refund for any change of plans. Postponements, rescheduled dates and cancellations follow the order terms and the organiser's decision. Read the terms for your specific order, and keep the confirmation until after the show. The [postponement and cancellation guide](/guides/what-to-do-if-a-concert-is-postponed-or-cancelled) explains how those outcomes differ.

## Which should you use?

- **If one of them is the official seller:** start there. SeatGeek is the primary seller for some events; check the artist, venue or promoter page to see who is named.
- **If both only have resale:** compare the exact seat, quantity, delivery estimate and complete current total on each site.
- **If the seats don't match:** don't call the lower number a saving. A worse view, a split pair or a later transfer can outweigh a small price gap.
- **If a feature matters to you:** Deal Score or Rewards can inform the choice, but only after the ticket itself matches.

## FAQ

**Is Vivid Seats cheaper than SeatGeek?**

Not consistently. Both are marketplaces where prices are set by sellers, and the answer changes with the event, the seat, the quantity and the moment you check. Compare complete current totals for matching tickets.

**Are Vivid Seats and SeatGeek the same company?**

No. They are separate ticketing businesses with their own sellers, fees, delivery rules and protection terms.

**Which has lower fees, Vivid Seats or SeatGeek?**

There's no event-independent answer. Both include mandatory fees in the upfront US price, but taxes, delivery and optional extras can differ by order. Compare the final order summaries.

**Why does SeatGeek have tickets when Vivid Seats doesn't, or the other way round?**

Each marketplace carries the listings its own sellers post, and SeatGeek may also be the primary seller for some events. Different inventory on each site is normal.

**Why is there no SeatGeek price on TourTicketCompare?**

SeatGeek's feed returns no prices to this site, so its button is a checked link without a figure. Vivid Seats supplies a listed price for many dates, which is why only one of the two buttons may show a number.

**Does Deal Score compare SeatGeek with Vivid Seats?**

No. Deal Score rates SeatGeek listings using SeatGeek's data. It isn't a cross-site price comparison.

**Which is more reliable, Vivid Seats or SeatGeek?**

Both publish order-protection terms, and reliability can't be reduced to one brand-level answer. Check the delivery estimate, transfer method and protection terms for the exact order.
