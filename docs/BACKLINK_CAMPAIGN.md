# Earned-backlink campaign

This is the operating runbook for the 90-day launch of `/guides/vivid-seats-vs-ticketmaster`. It permits earned editorial outreach only: no paid links, reciprocal links, bulk guest posting, exact-match anchor demands, or claims about live inventory and prices.

## Weekly commitment and targets

Budget 3–5 hours each week. By day 90, target 30 genuinely qualified contacts, at least 20 individual pitches, four relevant new referring domains, and two deep links to the comparison page. Track replies, placements, referring domains, referral visits, and assisted provider clicks; a contact count is not a result by itself.

## Weeks 1–2: finish the citation asset

- Complete human review of every cited primary source and publish the guide.
- Verify the event-pair module, methodology timestamp, eligible-event count, schema, and tracked CTAs in production.
- Capture one desktop and one mobile screenshot of the methodology/module area.
- Prepare a concise data summary: eligible event count, the gates applied, the data timestamp, and what the count does not claim.
- Prepare a one-paragraph pitch focused on the useful evidence, not the affiliate relationship.

Suggested pitch:

> We reviewed the current FTC, Ticketmaster, and Vivid Seats guidance and built a plain-English comparison of fees, transfers, delivery, and buyer protection. The page also publishes a timestamped count of upcoming events for which our existing reviewed data has a safe event-specific link on both services. It makes no inventory or price claim, but it gives readers a concrete way to understand why a Vivid Seats purchase may arrive through Ticketmaster and how to compare the same event.

## Prospect qualification

Build a list of 35–40 prospects across consumer-finance and ticket-fee reporters, live-music and entertainment publications, consumer-protection or library resource pages, venue/fan/event-planning sites with outdated ticket-buying resources, and journalists covering transfers or resale regulation.

A prospect qualifies only when all three are true:

1. The publication or page is relevant to ticket fees, ticket transfers, event planning, consumer protection, or live entertainment.
2. A specific article, resource page, or reporter beat creates a credible reason to cite this guide.
3. The pitch can name a useful angle for that recipient without asking for a prescribed anchor.

Reject generic directories, sites selling placements, unrelated high-authority domains, and anyone requiring a reciprocal link.

## Weeks 3–8: individual outreach

Send 4–6 individual pitches per week. Choose one angle per recipient: the reviewed exact-event count, the transfer-through-Ticketmaster explanation, or the primary-source fee/protection comparison. Mention the recipient's relevant article or beat and explain what the guide adds. Follow up once after 5–7 business days; stop after that unless the recipient replies.

## Weeks 6–10: resource and news outreach

- Find genuinely broken or materially outdated ticket-buying links on relevant resource pages and offer this guide as one possible replacement.
- Watch for ticket-fee, transfer, cancellation, or resale-regulation reporting where concise sourced commentary would help.
- Provide the primary-source link alongside any comment so the journalist can verify it independently.
- Never imply that the eligible-event count measures inventory, popularity, market share, or value.

## Weeks 9–12: refresh and repeat

Refresh the eligible-event count through the normal reviewed-data pipeline, verify that the cited policies have not changed, and repitch only recipients whose beat matches a new development. Record which angle earned replies or citations and turn the winning angle into a quarterly update.

## Tracking fields

Use one row per contact with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Prospect and domain | Deduplicate outreach and referring domains |
| Contact and role | Confirm the person owns the relevant beat/page |
| Relevant article/resource | Evidence that the contact is qualified |
| Angle | Dataset, transfer explanation, or sourced policy comparison |
| First pitch / follow-up dates | Enforce cadence and the one-follow-up limit |
| Status | Drafted, sent, replied, declined, placed, or closed |
| Placement URL and target URL | Confirm the link is live and deep-linked |
| Anchor used | Observe editorial wording; never prescribe it |
| Referral visits | First-party acquisition result |
| Assisted provider clicks | Outbound clicks whose landing journey began with that referral |
| Notes | Short factual context; no personal or sensitive data |

Review the tracker weekly. Count a referring domain only after the editorial link is live and crawlable. Recheck placements at day 30, day 60, and day 90.
